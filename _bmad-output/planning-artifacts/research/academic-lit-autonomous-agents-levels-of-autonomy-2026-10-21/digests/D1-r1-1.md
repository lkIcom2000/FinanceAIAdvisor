## Design relevance

- **Per-action autonomy ladder (Sheridan & Verplank 1978):** the five agent modes map closely onto the 10-level scale. *Inform* is roughly L1-2 (or L1-4 seen as decision support). *Recommend* is L4. *Execute after confirmation* is L5. *Execute within mandate with notification and undo* is L6-7 (the veto window plus "executes, then necessarily informs"). *Fully autonomous* is L8-10. This grounds the ladder in a canonical source. Note that the original scale has no explicit "undo" or "mandate" concept. These are the thesis's own extensions and should be presented as such.
- **Stage-specific automation (PSW 2000):** the agent can automate information acquisition and analysis highly (data aggregation, categorisation) while keeping decision selection and action implementation at lower levels for high-stakes actions. This justifies giving autonomy *per stage* and per action rather than one global autonomy setting.
- **Criteria for selecting the level (PSW 2000 secondary criteria):** "costs of decision/action consequences" directly supports the criteria *amount at stake*, *reversibility* and *regulatory class*. "Automation reliability" supports *data confidence*. The primary criteria (workload, SA, complacency, skill) support keeping the user informed through notifications even in mandate mode.
- **Mandate as delegated goal/intention (BDI, Rao & Georgeff 1991/1995; Wooldridge & Jennings 1995):** a user mandate can be modelled as an adopted intention with bounded commitment. The agent acts autonomously (weak-agency "autonomy" and "pro-activeness") only within the intention the user delegated. The BDI commitment and reconsideration idea supports revoking mandates and re-asking when beliefs (data confidence) change.
- **User override and trust (PSW 2008; Kaber 2018):** SA and trust calibration justify the notify-and-undo pattern and the user's ability to override. Kaber's critique (content unverified) should be cited as a caution that fixed LOA taxonomies are presumptive, which motivates the thesis's adaptive, criteria-driven assignment.
