## Design relevance
- **Bounded mandate as a delegation contract (Jensen & Meckling; Eisenhardt):** The mandate (keep the account above 0 EUR, max 300 EUR per transfer) is an explicit contract that delegates limited decision authority. Its bounds limit residual loss when the agent's behaviour diverges from the user's interests. Eisenhardt's view that outcome uncertainty and information systems shape contract design supports making the scope narrow and observable.
- **Notifications, activity log, undo and revoke as monitoring and bonding:** Notifications and logs are cheap monitoring that reduce information asymmetry. One-tap undo and revocation act as the agent's bonding commitment and cap residual loss. Keeping these costs near zero matters most for low-literacy users, who cannot audit actions in detail.
- **Calibrated trust (Lee & See):** Show performance (what the agent did and how it turned out), process (the rule it followed, i.e. the mandate) and purpose (it acts for the user, not the bank). The aim is calibrated reliance, avoiding both misuse (overtrust) and disuse.
- **User-adjustable parameters against algorithm aversion (Dietvorst et al. 2015, 2018):** Seeing an agent err erodes trust quickly, so first errors need special handling: undo, plain-language explanation, no hidden actions. Letting users even slightly change the mandate parameters (threshold, cap) is empirically linked to higher willingness to use imperfect algorithms. Logg et al. (2019) is a counterpoint: lay people may over-rely on algorithms, which strengthens the case for confirmation gates on large actions.
- **Confirm vs. act (Horvitz 1999):** A decision-theoretic split between acting autonomously, asking (dialog) and doing nothing, based on uncertainty and the cost of a poor guess, maps onto the rules "small and reversible inside the mandate = act and notify" and "large or irreversible = ask". Easy invocation and dismissal map onto one-tap undo. (Detailed principle wording is unverified this run.)
- **Pre-commitment in a calm moment (Thaler & Benartzi; Madrian & Shea):** SMarT shows that advance commitment with a cap and a free opt-out can change behaviour. Auto-enrollment shows that defaults are sticky and read as implicit advice, so mandate defaults carry an advice-like responsibility. Caveat: DOL CLEAR rates SMarT's causal evidence as low.
- **Robo-advisory framing (Jung et al. 2018; Cardillo & Chiappini 2024):** These place the artefact within automated financial advisory research. The 2018 Electronic Markets paper on low-budget, risk-averse consumers is the closest prior DSR artefact to the target user group.
