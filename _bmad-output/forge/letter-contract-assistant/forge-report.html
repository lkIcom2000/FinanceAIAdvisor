Forge report · Fine (master's thesis, DSR)

Letter & Contract Assistant

The document becomes an untrusted input channel and a Trigger source. The LLM extracts, a deterministic grounding check verifies, the customer confirms, and only then does the Core compute.

Locked decisions

  • Mandatory slice UJ-7: Lena's car-insurance premium increase. Fact card with source highlights → confirm → explanation with forecast impact → deadline Notification → special-termination template (L1). An objection letter is refused transparently.
  • Stretch: insurance policy (closes UJ-4), then dunning letter plus instalment request.
  • Text PDFs only, extracted deterministically. Scans and photos are rejected.
  • Pipeline: redaction → NLU extraction → span-grounding check → confirmation → explanation through the Check Chain. There is no path from a document to an Action.
  • Gate: confirming facts is a confirm control, a deadline becomes the Trigger DOCUMENT_DEADLINE, a letter is a deterministic template at L1, Fine never sends, and a new criterion legal caps at L1.
  • Module documents with DocumentFactsPort. Facts become expected cash flows with their own provId. The audit stores hashes only, never the original text.
  • GDPR: the customer deletes, and there is no automatic retention period.
  • Evaluation: SM-10a ungrounded values = 0%; field accuracy and misassignment are reported; SM-5 and SM-7 are extended; there is no E5. The UAT includes a deliberately wrong field on the fact card.

Rejected and why

  • Paperless office (filing, search, task manager): it is administration, not advice → outlook.
  • OCR and a vision model: a second model family and confounded error sources → outlook.
  • Objection letters: a legal assessment of the individual case is a legal service under the RDG.
  • LLM-written letters: they lose the Smartlaw argument.
  • Sending by Fine: it creates an outbound channel and liability.
  • Utility bills and pension notices: tenancy and social law, and long documents.
  • Facts in bank.contracts: provenance blurs and AD-12 breaks.
  • Original text in the audit: it conflicts with GDPR Art. 17.

Weak points that survived

  • A value can be grounded and still assigned to the wrong field. Only the human confirmation catches it, and automation bias may defeat that (tested in the UAT).
  • The RDG reading is the author's own, not a legal opinion.
  • The phone-photo case, the most realistic one for C-class customers, is not covered.
  • Redaction of user-uploaded PDFs misses unknown third-party names.
  • Letter language, German or English, is still open.
